TOEIC EXAM STRUCTURE AND THE VIETNAMESE STUDENT CONTEXT

WHAT TOEIC IS IN VIETNAM

The TOEIC is not a school subject. In Vietnam it functions as a graduation gate, a job filter, and a proxy signal for employability. Most universities require a minimum TOEIC score to graduate.

Typical thresholds:
450 to 550 is basic graduation level.
600 to 750 is considered a good graduate.
800 or above is considered strong and competitive.

The key insight: TOEIC is designed to measure recognition, not production. Students learn to recognise English, not use it under pressure.

TEST STRUCTURE

The standard TOEIC used in Vietnam is the TOEIC Listening and Reading test. Total score: 990 points.

LISTENING SECTION - 495 points - 4 parts

Part 1 - Photographs - 6 questions
Look at a picture and choose the correct sentence describing it.
Tests basic vocabulary and prepositions.

Part 2 - Question-Response - 25 questions
Hear a question and choose the best reply.
No speaking required. This is one of the biggest score swing areas.

Part 3 - Short Conversations - 39 questions
Listen to a dialogue and answer 3 questions about it.
Tests passive listening, keyword recognition, guessing from context.

Part 4 - Short Talks - 30 questions
Listen to announcements and business talks and answer questions.
Same skills as Part 3.

READING SECTION - 495 points - 3 parts

Part 5 - Incomplete Sentences - 30 questions
Choose the correct word or phrase to complete a sentence.
Tests grammar patterns and vocabulary.

Part 6 - Text Completion - 16 questions
Fill in blanks in emails and memos.
Tests grammar plus flow and context.

Part 7 - Reading Comprehension - 54 questions
Read emails, notices, and articles and answer questions.
Includes single, double, and triple passages.
Tests scanning, locating evidence, and paraphrase recognition.

WHAT TOEIC DOES NOT TEST

TOEIC does not test speaking, thinking speed, real conversation, handling pressure, explaining ideas, or negotiation.

HOW VIETNAMESE STUDENTS TYPICALLY STUDY

The system becomes pattern training, not language development. Students memorise question types, recognise traps, practise elimination strategies, and learn fixed grammar rules.

Common mindset: "If I see although, choose a contrast answer." "Listen for keywords, not meaning."

WHY STUDENTS LOSE MARKS

Most Vietnamese students understand 70 to 80 percent of the content but lose marks on traps, timing, and hesitation. TOEIC rewards fast and confident pattern recognition, not deep language mastery.

THE CORE COACHING INSIGHT

TOEIC trains recognition, pattern matching, and exam strategy. The coach's job is to diagnose which patterns the student is missing and drill those patterns until they are automatic.
