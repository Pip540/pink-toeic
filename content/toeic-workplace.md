# TOEIC in the Workplace — Why It Matters Across Asia

The TOEIC system is widely used across Asia, often more than in Europe or the UK.

The important distinction: it is not just an exam. In many Asian countries it has become a gatekeeping tool for employment and graduation, especially in corporate and technical sectors.


## Where TOEIC is widely used

### Japan

One of the biggest TOEIC markets in the world. Used heavily by companies like Rakuten and Toyota. Employees often need a minimum TOEIC score for promotion. Some universities require TOEIC for graduation.

Reality: Many candidates can score high but still struggle in real conversations — the gap between test performance and actual communication ability is significant.


### South Korea

TOEIC is deeply embedded in hiring culture. Required for jobs at companies like Samsung and Hyundai. Scores are often listed directly on CVs.

This creates a high-pressure exam economy focused on test scores rather than real communication ability.


### Vietnam

Used by universities including FPT University. Graduation often requires TOEIC 450 to 750 depending on the program. Companies use it as a baseline filter for hiring.

Students pass TOEIC but cannot function in real workplace English. This is the core problem Pink addresses.


### Thailand

Used in government jobs and airlines. Important for roles in tourism and international business.


### Taiwan

Widely used in universities and corporate hiring. Seen as a standardised benchmark of global readiness.


### China

Less dominant than local exams such as CET-4 and CET-6. Used mainly in multinational companies.


## The Underlying Problem

Across all these countries, the pattern is identical.

TOEIC measures recognition, not production. Students learn grammar patterns and listening tricks. But they do not learn to think in English, respond under pressure, or explain ideas clearly.

Result: a generation of graduates who can decode English but cannot use English as a working tool.


## Strategic Insight

TOEIC has become a signal of compliance rather than competence.

That gap is exactly where Pink sits. Instead of replacing TOEIC, Pink converts TOEIC scores into real-world ability — training both exam passing and real communication simultaneously.

TOEIC score thresholds by country and what they mean in practice:

- South Korea: 700+ often required for Samsung, Hyundai, and similar employers
